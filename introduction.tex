\section{Introduction}

In recent years, \gls{AI}, more specifically \gls{ML}, methods have become an
important research subject to most subfields of nuclear science and engineering,
such as in-core fuel management, reactor safety, flow regime prediction, and
radiation protection, to achieve an optimal core loading map, to make nuclear
reactors safer, to quickly comprehend flow data, and to accurately identify
critical isotopes in nuclear materials. Two recently published review papers
\cite{Gomez2020, Nissan2019} pointed out the potential, competence, and
importance of these methods. Different optimization techniques predominantly
based on iterative approaches like \gls{GA} and individual parameter search have
been successfully applied to design a reactor core. Of the recent studies, Wei
\emph{et al.} \cite{Wei2018} investigated several critical feature parameters of
a \gls{MSR} channel by parameter interpolation using KENO-VI in SCALE 6.1. In
the study, the critical feature parameters are the fuel material, Li-6
concentration, composition of fuel material, channel geometry, moderator,
moderator density and salt operation temperature. The research showed that Li-6
has a strong effect on the criticality. In all channel geometries, the breaking
point for the multiplication factor was observed at 3-to-5 for the ratio of
pitch to fuel channel diameter and in the interval of 0.05 and 0.1 for the fuel
volume fraction. Increasing the amount of moderator resulted in an increase in
multiplication factor, but then further increase caused a decrease. The effect
of temperature coefficient changes from negative to positive after 12 cm with
increasing pitch length. Likewise, Anderson \emph{et al.} \cite{Anderson2019}
assessed the importance of several parameters (fissile enrichment, lattice
pitch, salt-moderator ratio and number of fuel channels) in the \gls{MSR} core
by looking into the change of the fission and removal transition matrices.
According to the results, the transition matrix is affected negatively with
increasing lattice pitch and salt-to-moderator ratio and positively with
increasing enrichment and burnup. The common deficiency of both studies is the
neglect of strong interdependence of the interested parameters. The effect of a
single parameter on performance metrics (multiplication factor, conversion
ratio, reactivity feedback coefficient, etc.) can be either positive or negative
or rarely fruitless. But, as to more than one parameter, the combined effect is
much more complicated than anticipated and can be seen in many ways: super
positioning, canceling out another, or acting on each other up to some extent.
Due to the above reasons, such multi-parameter optimizations would require a bit
more sophisticated searching techniques like \gls{GA}, Particle Swarm, and
\gls{ANN}. Unlike parametric study, these sophisticated optimization techniques
are very useful in finding optimal parameters, particularly for multi-objective
problems and are the most common and effective way of determining optimum core
loading maps \cite{Nissan2019}. Core designs based on evolutionary algorithms
are relatively new and have been recently achieved by many researchers. Kumar
and Tsvetkov \cite{Kumar2015} used the \gls{GA} coupled to Regression Splines to
optimize several performance metrics such as multiplication factors, fast
fission factor, thermal efficiency, and burnup in the design of the Gas Cooled
Fast Breeder reactor. This method relies on the interpolation of intermediate
values of the parameters and metrics. For a fuel pin cell, the optimum value of
the radius, enrichment fraction, mass flow rate, and the coolant temperature at
the inlet was found to be 0.22 cm, 18.5 wt. \%, 63.5  $^o$C, and 35.9 kg/s,
respectively. Zeng \emph{et al.} \cite{Zeng2020} performed a multi-objective
optimization to the Advanced Burner Test Reactor (ABTR) core, a sodium-cooled
fast test reactor (SFR), to get a final core design. As an optimizer, an
integrated version of the US Department of Energy's Nuclear Energy Advanced
Modeling and Simulation (NEAMS) Workbench along with \gls{GA} was used for core
modeling. Although they found about 3150 optimal core designs within the Pareto
front solutions by optimizing the reactivity swing, Pu mass feed, core volume,
core power, and peak fast flux, only six candidate designs passed their
performance criteria based on the constraints defined. Regarding the use of
\gls{ML} methods in miscellaneous reactor applications, Bae \emph{et al.}
\cite{Bae2020} successfully predicted Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR) \gls{UNF}
composition by using the Keras neural network. They showed that their proposed
model outperforms the average recipe method, one of the transmutation methods
which supplies neutronic calculations externally to the nuclear fuel cycle
simulators, by yielding less than 1\% error for the \gls{UNF} inventory decay
heat and activity and less than 5\% error for the important isotopes. In the
case of designing a nuclear reactor, recently published researches
\cite{Kim2018, Kim2020} focused on the assembly and core design at the Kyoto
University Critical Assembly by means of \gls{ANN} coupled to MCNP6. In these
works, various core materials, numbers of fuel regions, and fuel assembly types
were defined as vital feature parameters whereas fast flux and multiplication
factor were the target metrics to be optimized. The used method looks new, but
it is nonetheless very similar to fuel loading pattern optimization studies from
the viewpoint of optimization technique simply by imitating conventional design
procedure.  
As discussed in detail, parametric studies which handle all feature variables
independently ignore complex interdependence of these variables and evaluate
performance metrics of a design by changing only one of the future variables at
a time, thus fixing others. Likewise, evolutionary algorithms deeply suffer from
the optimization parameters of optimizers such as population size, number of
generations and cross-over rate due to the indeterminacy of these parameters
prior to evaluations, leading to more computational resources than required.
Furthermore, optimization methods frequently converge to local instead of global
optima in addition to lacking the ability to achieve ideal designs that are
intuitively eliminated by the optimizer's elimination and/or selection criteria.
At this point, the use of the learning-based methods seems quite rational for
the design of a reactor because of their exceptional ability to scan every inch
of the parameter space without missing the global optima. The \gls{ML} methods,
which have not been considered for the sole design of a reactor core so far,
will be the best option in this regard. Accordingly, we recommend using
ultramodern \gls{ML} methods to estimate the optimum values of feature
parameters of a reactor based on the prediction of performance metrics without
running a reactor physics code for neutron transport calculations. This research
would make an outstanding contribution to the further development of core
optimization studies as the proposed method is superior to other optimization
techniques in predicting performance metrics accurately and quickly.
