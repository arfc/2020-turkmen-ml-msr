\section{Introduction}

In recent years, \gls{AI}, more specifically \gls{ML}, methods have become
important to most subfields of nuclear science and engineering, such as in-core
fuel management, reactor safety, flow regime prediction, and radiation
protection, to achieve an optimal core loading map, to make nuclear reactors
safer, to quickly comprehend flow data, and to accurately identify critical
isotopes in nuclear materials. Two recently published review papers
\cite{Gomez2020, Nissan2019} pointed out the potential, competence, and
importance of these methods. Different optimization techniques predominantly
based on iterative approaches like \gls{GA}s and individual parameter searches
have been successfully applied to design a reactor core.

Among these recent studies, Wei \emph{et al.} \cite{Wei2018} investigated
several critical feature parameters of a \gls{MSR} channel by parameter
interpolation using KENO-VI in SCALE 6.1 \cite{scale2020}. In the study, the
critical feature parameters are the fuel material, $^{6}$Li concentration,
composition of fuel material, channel geometry, moderator, moderator density and
salt operation temperature. The research showed that $^{6}$Li has a strong
effect on the criticality. In all different shapes of fuel channels, Wei
\emph{et al.} \cite{Wei2018} observed that the turning point from
under-moderation to over-moderation for the multiplication factor ($k$) is at a
pitch to channel ratio ($P/D$) between 2 and 4, and a fuel volume fraction
between 0 and 0.1. Increasing the amount of moderator resulted in an increase in
$k$, but then further increase caused a decrease. Meanwhile, the temperature
coefficient changes from negative to positive for pitch $>$ 12 cm.

Likewise, Anderson \emph{et al.} \cite{Anderson2019} assessed the importance of
several parameters (fissile enrichment, lattice pitch, salt-moderator ratio and
number of fuel channels) in the \gls{MSR} core by looking into the change of the
fission and removal transition matrices. In that work, it was understood that
the change in the transition matrix increases with increasing salt-to-moderator
ratio, and decreases with increasing enrichment and lattice pitch. But the
effect of burnup varies depending on which parameter it is used with.

The common deficiency of both studies is the neglect of strong interdependence
of the investigated parameters. The effect of a single parameter on performance
metrics (multiplication factor, conversion ratio, reactivity feedback
coefficient, etc.) can be either beneficial or detrimental (or rarely
fruitless). As for more than one parameter, correlations between parameters can
be seen as super positioning, canceling out another, or acting on each other up
to some extent. Correspondingly, such multi-parameter optimizations would
require more sophisticated searching techniques like \gls{GA}, Particle Swarm,
and \gls{ANN}. Unlike parametric study, these sophisticated optimization
techniques are very useful in finding optimal parameters, particularly for
multi-objective problems and are a common and effective way of determining
optimal core loading maps \cite{Nissan2019}.

Core designs based on evolutionary algorithms are relatively new and have been
recently achieved by many researchers. Kumar and Tsvetkov \cite{Kumar2015} used
a Genetic Algorithm method coupled to Regression Splines to optimize several
performance metrics such as multiplication factor, fast fission factor, thermal
efficiency, and burnup in the design of the Gas Cooled Fast Breeder reactor.
This method interpolates intermediate values of the parameters and metrics. For
a fuel pin cell, the optimal values of the radius, enrichment fraction, mass
flow rate, and coolant temperature at the inlet were found to be 0.22 cm, 18.5
wt. \%, 63.5  $^o$C, and 35.9 kg/s, respectively. Zeng \emph{et al.}
\cite{Zeng2020} performed a multi-objective optimization to the Advanced Burner
Test Reactor (ABTR) core, a sodium-cooled fast test reactor (SFR), to get a
final core design. As an optimizer, an integrated version of the US Department
of Energy's Nuclear Energy Advanced Modeling and Simulation (NEAMS) Workbench
\cite{neams2019} along with \gls{GA} was used for core modeling. Although they
found about 3150 optimal core designs within Pareto front solutions by
optimizing the reactivity swing, Pu mass feed, core volume, core power, and peak
fast flux, only six candidate designs passed their performance criteria based on
the defined constraints.

Regarding the use of \gls{ML} methods in miscellaneous reactor applications, Bae
\emph{et al.} \cite{Bae2020} successfully predicted Pressurized Water Reactor
(PWR) \gls{UNF} composition by training the dense neural network in the Keras
\cite{keras2015}. They showed that their proposed model outperforms the average
recipe method, one of the transmutation methods which supplies neutronic
calculations externally to the nuclear fuel cycle simulators, by yielding less
than 1\% error for the \gls{UNF} inventory decay heat and activity and less than
5\% error for the important isotopes.

Recently published studies \cite{Kim2018, Kim2020} reported the results of the
assembly and core design at the Kyoto University Critical Assembly by means of
an \gls{ANN} coupled to MCNP6 \cite{mcnp2017}. These studies are similar to fuel
loading pattern optimization studies in terms of optimization method. In these
works, various core materials, numbers of fuel regions, and fuel assembly types
were defined as vital feature parameters whereas fast flux and multiplication
factor were the target metrics to be optimized.

As discussed in detail, parametric studies which handle all feature variables
independently ignore complex interdependence of these variables and evaluate
performance metrics of a design by changing only one of the feature variables at
a time, thus fixing others. Likewise, evolutionary algorithms suffer from the
choices of optimizer hyper-parameters such as population size, number of
generations and cross-over rate, since it is unlikely to determine exactly what
these parameters would be without making a preliminary calculation. This hence
results in excessive usage of computational resources. Furthermore, optimization
methods frequently converge to local instead of global optima fail to achieve
ideal designs that are eliminated by the optimizer's elimination and/or
selection criteria. At this point, the use of learning-based methods seems
rational for the design of a reactor because of their exceptional ability to
fully scan every inch of the parameter space without missing the global optimum.

The \gls{ML} methods, which have not been considered for the sole design of a
reactor core so far, will be the best option in this regard. Accordingly, we
recommend using \gls{ML} methods to estimate the optimal values of reactor
design parameters by predicting performance metrics without running neutron
transport calculations. This research demonstrate that the proposed method
predicts performance metrics more accurately and quickly than current
optimization techniques.
